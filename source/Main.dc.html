How to study

Use AI as a tutor

AI helps when it explains things and gets in the way when it does the work for you. Two recent studies found the same pattern, so these six habits keep the thinking on your side.

  1. 1

    Write it yourself first

    Do course exercises with AI autocomplete turned off. Typing the code is part of learning it.

  2. 2

    Ask for hints, not fixes

    When you're stuck, ask why something works or for one hint. Don't paste the task in and copy the answer.

  3. 3

    Debug before you ask

    Read the error, guess the cause and test your guess. In the coding study, debugging showed the biggest gap.

  4. 4

    Let AI review, not write

    Once your code works, ask AI to review it and explain what it would change and why.

  5. 5

    Rebuild from a blank file

    After each island's project, rebuild the core part without looking at your old code.

  6. 6

    Explain it back

    Every island ends with questions. Answer out loud or on paper before you show the model answer, and count it only if you had it.

Coding study · Anthropic, January 2026

50% vs 67%

Quiz scores of 52 mostly junior developers who learned a new Python library with an AI assistant, against those who coded by hand. The AI group wasn't significantly faster. Those who used AI to ask for explanations scored about as well as the hand coders.

Read the study

Math study · PNAS, 2025

17% lower

Exam grades of high school students after their ChatGPT-style assistant was taken away, against students who never had one, even though it had raised their practice grades by 48%. A tutor version with safeguards largely avoided the drop.

How to use this map

1

Take the "Start here" picks first. The other courses and books are backups for when the first pick doesn't click.

2

Build the project before you move on. Following a course and doing the work yourself are different skills.

3

Most islands have a free option. "Free to audit" means the videos are free and certificates or graded labs cost extra. Coursera replaced free auditing with a free first-module preview in 2025, so Coursera courses here are marked paid.

How the numbers were made. Hours are my estimates: each island's main course at its official length (for example, about 95 hours for the Machine Learning Specialization and about 94 for the math specialization) plus time for the project. Where a course states no length, the hours are my own estimate. Months assume your chosen pace and about 4.3 weeks a month. Effort dots are my judgment. The "Am I ready?" lines paraphrase each course's own page. Demand figures quote the linked reports, and GPU prices were checked on provider pages in November 2026: an H100 rents for about $2.69 to $4.89 an hour (RunPod, Lambda). DeepLearning.AI's Pro plan, which unlocks certificates and graded work, costs $25 a month billed yearly or $30 monthly. Udemy and Coursera prices vary by country and sale, so they're only marked paid.

Links go to official course pages, publisher pages and author sites, checked in November 2026. Your progress is saved in this browser only. If a link breaks, search the title on the provider's site.
